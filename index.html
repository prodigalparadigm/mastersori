The un-training

First, undo
“don’t touch.”

For thirty years, those of us in IT taught people not to touch. Stay in the browser. Stay in the inbox. If something breaks, call someone.

Then AI arrived and asked them to do the opposite: explore, connect, try, break, fix, and build. The problem is not simply that people need a better tutorial. It is that they learned the old lesson very well.

Enablement is not teaching a tool.
It is giving people room to act.

Mastersori is my method for doing that deliberately.
Kathleen Bartin · Prodigal Paradigm

Inside the method
01 / Prepare

Make the ground safe.

Listen across the organization. Map the people and departments. Turn valid objections into usable governance, give the ambition a timeline, and win the department heads before any classroom.

The survey · The sort · Governance first · The timeline · The heads

02 / Begin

Make it personal.

Set everyone up the same way, then let them build something useful from their own real work in the first session. Send them home with the project they thought was out of reach.

Wheels up · The moonshot homework

03 / Continue

Make it their own.

Give every person a rung to stand on. Find the one who is ahead and wants to share it. Then leave in a way that keeps people wired to each other.

The name is
an argument.

Master+sori

Margaret Masterman put meaning at the heart of early machine translation. Maria Montessori built an educational method around a prepared environment and the learner’s growing independence.

The connection is my own: prepare the conditions, trust people to act, and help them express what they mean well enough to build it.

This is a dedication, not sainthood. The complications belong in the story, too.
